1. Data Access

All hackathon data will be in your team’s EODH workspace, ready to open in a Python Jupyter notebook. Set up your account by following Getting Started with EODH before the 2 December session. The EODH platform does not display uploaded datasets as maps, so plan to view them in a notebook.

Tip

Starter notebooks
Example Python notebooks for loading and exploring these datasets are in the tutorial/ folder of the GitHub repository. See Hackathon notebooks and data for the full list.

Imago’s UK-wide products can also be browsed in the Imago Data Catalogue, currently a preview release.

2. Datasets

2.1 Imago products

Imago produces satellite-derived data products for social science and policy research, summarised to small areas so they can be joined directly to population and deprivation data.

  • Land surface temperature heat indicators, Sentinel-3 (MSOA). Summer heat indicators for every MSOA in the UK, built from Sentinel-3 land surface temperature (~1 km pixels), 2019–2024. Summer night-time mean and median are being finalised, and daytime mean and median will be added. The underlying Sentinel-3 product is in the Imago Data Catalogue.
  • Land surface temperature heat indicators, Sentinel-3 (LSOA). The same indicators at LSOA level, prepared for the hackathon. In dense urban areas many LSOAs are smaller than a single 1 km pixel, so neighbouring LSOAs can share the same value. Comparing this with the 100 m product below is a useful exercise in its own right.
  • Land surface temperature heat indicators, 100 m (LSOA). An educational product built by Imago for the hackathon from the NCEO 100 m land surface temperature data (section 2.2), 2020–2024. Summer night-time mean and median, with daytime mean and median to follow. The finer source resolution captures street- and neighbourhood-level differences that the 1 km product misses.
  • Air temperature indicators, HadUK-Grid (MSOA). Met Office 1 km gridded air temperature, 2015–2024, summarised into annual and seasonal indicators and heat and cold extremes. Use these to compare surface with air temperature, or to bring in cold extremes alongside heat.
  • Greenspace (LSOA). A UK-wide greenspace product based on a binary green / non-green classification of satellite imagery, summarised to LSOA.
Tip

Land surface temperature is not air temperature. Land surface temperature measures how hot the ground and roofs are; air temperature is closer to what people feel. The two are related but can differ a lot, especially by day. The hackathon gives you both.

2.2 Pixel-level Earth observation data

For teams who want to build their own indicators or go below LSOA level.

  • Land surface temperature, 100 m (NCEO). Gridded land surface temperature at 100 m from the National Centre for Earth Observation’s Surface Temperature Group at the University of Leicester. Daily day and night files (NetCDF), 2020–2024, on a latitude/longitude grid (EPSG:4326), covering the North East (bounding box −2.69, 54.45 to −1.24, 55.81). Values are in Kelvin: subtract 273.15 for °C. Reproject to British National Grid (EPSG:27700) before measuring distances or areas.
  • Sentinel-2 (EODH). Open 10 m multispectral imagery from the EODH catalogue, provided as a single cloud-free summer composite matched to the land surface temperature period. Use the red and near-infrared bands to compute vegetation indices such as NDVI. Newcastle imagery for summers 2019–2026 is also discoverable in the EODH catalogue if you want to go further.
Important

Credit and use. Any output using the 100 m land surface temperature data, or the Imago 100 m LSOA product derived from it, must credit the NCEO Leicester Surface Temperature Group. These data are provided for use in the hackathon workspaces only and must not be shared outside the event.

2.3 Supporting layers

Loaded for every team as context:

  • LSOA and MSOA boundaries (2021), from the Imago Data Catalogue: LSOA boundaries for the United Kingdom (2021). A North East-only copy, ne_lsoa_2021_boundaries.gpkg, is in the repository’s data/ folder.
  • Population and deprivation tables for the North East, to show who lives in the hottest, coldest and least green places:
    • ne_lsoa_population_imd.csv (1,257 LSOAs): Census 2021 population, residents aged 0–4, 65+ and 75+, household tenure (owned, social rented, private rented), and the Index of Multiple Deprivation 2025 score, rank and decile, with the Income, Health, Living Environment and older-people income deprivation domains.
    • ne_msoa_population_imd.csv (253 MSOAs): the same aggregated to MSOA, with population-weighted deprivation scores and the share of residents in England’s most deprived 20% of LSOAs.
    • ne_population_imd_variables.csv: a description of every column. Full definitions are in data/VARIABLES.md.

These tables are in the data/ folder of the repository and will be in your workspace. The Python notebook that builds them, with step-by-step notes, is in scripts/ if you want to see how they were made. Its last two sections have starter plots and worked examples of merging these tables with the Imago products.
