The Hackathon Day

Wednesday 9 December 2026 · 10:00–16:00 · Newcastle, UK — venue TBC

A collaborative, fast-paced day working in small interdisciplinary teams on real-world local challenges, using Earth observation products from Imago and the EODH.

Important

Before the day
All participants should attend the two preparation meetings: the team meeting on Monday 30 November and the EODH workspace introduction on Wednesday 2 December. Arriving with your team formed, your problem statement in hand and your EODH workspace working is what makes a six-hour hackathon possible.

Session details

10:00–10:30 — Welcome and framing

Why Earth observation for local policy, what Imago and the EODH provide, and how the day connects to the North East’s priorities on heat and greenspace.

10:30–12:30 — Sprint 1

Teams agree a focused question within their problem statement, explore the data in their EODH workspace and start building. Mentors from Imago and the EODH circulate to help with data access and methods; policy participants help keep the work anchored to a real decision.

13:15–13:30 — Mid-point stand-up

Each team gives a two-minute update: what question you’re answering, what’s working and what’s blocking you.

13:30–15:00 — Sprint 2

Finish the analysis, make the output readable for a non-technical audience and prepare a short presentation.

15:00–16:00 — Presentations and close

Each team presents for 5 minutes with 2 minutes of questions. Policy partners reflect on which outputs feel most useful and how they might be taken forward.

Mentors and judges

Mentors. Each team is supported through the day by mentors from Imago and the EODH, who help with data access, methods and code, and by policy mentors who help keep the work anchored to a real decision. Mentors also join the team meeting on 30 November and the EODH session on 2 December.

Judges. A small panel of policy and technical partners will watch the presentations and give feedback. They will look for:

  • Relevance: does the output answer a question a council or public health team actually needs answered?
  • Use of the data: is the data used sensibly, at a scale that suits the question?
  • Honesty: are the limits of the data and method clear?
  • Clarity: could a non-technical decision-maker understand and act on it?

Names of mentors and judges will be announced before the event.

What to bring

  • A laptop and charger (technical participants)
  • Your EODH login set up at the 2 December session
  • Any local knowledge, documents or policy questions relevant to your problem statement (policy participants)
